# Offset Disambiguation — Wang (Shazam) and Gururani & Lerch (ISMIR 2017) vs. our measured sync failures · 2026-07-28

**Scope:** two primary sources, read in full from their own PDFs, assessed against three failure classes measured in [field-test-8-results.md](field-test-8-results.md) and [ux-notes-2026-07-28.md](ux-notes-2026-07-28.md) — harmonic comb ambiguity (a), self-match after room discontinuity (b), deadband-locked residuals (c). Every claim below cites the section/figure it came from; nothing is inferred beyond the retrieved text. Where a paper is silent on something we need, that is stated explicitly in §3.

---

## 1. What each paper actually is

### 1a. Avery Li-Chun Wang, "An Industrial-Strength Audio Search Algorithm," Shazam Entertainment Ltd., ICMIR 2003 (pp. 7–13; this is the paper docs/recognition-audit-2026.md's ACRCloud/ShazamKit comparison sits downstream of)

Retrieved in full from `https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/papers/Wang03-shazam.pdf` (7 pages, all text and figures legible). Confirmed byline: "Avery Li-Chun Wang, avery@shazamteam.com, Shazam Entertainment, Ltd."; abstract states the system was live "in Germany, Finland, and the UK" against "over 1.8M tracks" (§1).

**Core mechanism, in three parts (§2):**

1. **Robust constellations (§2.1).** A time-frequency point is a candidate peak "if it has a higher energy content than all its neighbors in a region centered around the point," chosen by a density criterion for uniform time-frequency coverage and by amplitude ("the highest amplitude peaks are most likely to survive" distortion). This reduces a spectrogram (Fig. 1A) to a sparse "constellation map" (Fig. 1B) — amplitude is discarded, only (time, frequency) coordinates survive. The paper's own framing: matching is "a kind of 'astronavigation,' in which a small patch of time-frequency constellation points must be quickly located within a large universe of points."

2. **Fast combinatorial hashing (§2.2).** Raw constellation points have low entropy — "a 1024-bin frequency axis yields only at most 10 bits of frequency data per peak." Anchor points are each paired with points inside a target zone (Fig. 1C); each pair yields a hash of **two frequency components plus the time delta between them**, packed into a 32-bit integer (Fig. 1D: `Hash:time = [f1:f2:Δt]:t1`), stored alongside the anchor's offset from file start and track ID in a 64-bit struct, sorted by hash value for fast lookup. This raises specificity "about a million times" over single-point matching (30 bits vs 10), at a quantified cost: with fan-out `F`, storage grows ~10x but search speeds up ~10,000x, and hash-survival probability degrades from `p` to approximately `p·[1-(1-p)^F]` — the paper works the algebra for `F=10` explicitly.

3. **Searching and scoring (§2.3).** Every sample hash that matches a database hash contributes a `(t_sample, t_database)` time pair, binned by track ID. Within a bin, true matches show as **a diagonal line in the scatterplot** (`t_database = t_sample + offset`, Fig. 3A) versus no structure for a non-match (Fig. 2A). Detection is done cheaply — not via a Hough transform ("overly general, computationally expensive, and susceptible to outliers") — but by computing `δt_k = t_k' - t_k` for every matched pair and **histogramming the δt values**: a real match produces a sharp histogram peak (Fig. 3B) versus a flat spread for noise (Fig. 2B). §2.3.1 turns this into a formal test: "the score is simply the number of matching and time-aligned hash tokens," and a **false-positive-rate threshold is set from the distribution of the highest score among incorrectly-matching tracks** in the database.

Robustness data (§3.1, Figs. 4–5): 50%-recognition SNR thresholds of roughly −9/−6/−3 dB for 15/10/5 s clean-noise samples, worse after simulated GSM 6.10 compression. §3.1 also names **"transparency"**: "even with a large database, we can correctly identify each of several tracks mixed together, including multiple versions of the same piece." §3.3 flags a specificity property directly relevant to us: "the algorithm is... very sensitive to which particular version of a track has been sampled" — it does not generalize across near-duplicate audio, it discriminates it.

### 1b. Siddharth Gururani & Alexander Lerch, "Automatic Sample Detection in Polyphonic Music," Georgia Institute of Technology Center for Music Technology — **Proceedings of the 18th ISMIR Conference, Suzhou, China, October 23–27, 2017, pp. 264–271** (this is ISMIR 2017 paper 118)

Retrieved in full from `https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000118.pdf` (8 pages). This is **not** a sync/offset-ambiguity paper by design — it is a plagiarism/musicology tool for detecting whether a short audio *sample* (in the hip-hop "digital sampling" sense) appears inside a longer polyphonic *song*, and where. Confirmed via abstract and footer attribution line on p.264.

**Core mechanism (§3, Fig. 1 block diagram):**

1. **Partially-fixed NMF (§3.1).** The query sample's magnitude spectrogram is factorized `V ≈ W·H` into `K=10` fixed spectral templates `W_o` and activations `H_o`. The candidate song is then factorized with **partially-fixed NMF (PFNMF)**: the template matrix is `[W_o | W_m]` where `W_o` is frozen (from the sample) and `W_m` (`L=20` templates) is learned freely to explain everything else in the mix. Only the resulting activations `H_s` — how strongly the sample's *frozen* templates fire inside the song — are used downstream. Pitch-shift robustness (57.5% of their corpus is pitch-shifted, §3.2.2) is handled by re-deriving `W_o^p` for 12 hypothesized semitone shifts and running PFNMF once per hypothesis, keeping only the minimum-cost candidate (§3.2.3).

2. **Subsequence DTW with a non-standard cost-matrix initialization (§3.2.2, Eq. 2, Fig. 3–5).** Because a sample can occur *multiple times* in a song (their Fig. 3 explicitly shows **a looped sample** — the same structural situation as our repetitive-material problem, but for a different purpose), a standard single-global-path DTW is wrong. They initialize the cost matrix to accumulate only along the sample's dimension (`C(1,j)=D(1,j)`, `C(i,1)=Σ D(k,i)`), which yields a **backtracking path from every frame in the last row** rather than one path from one corner. This produces two derived signals: the **DTW cost function** (Fig. 4, one value per song-frame, local minima = candidate sample end points) and the **DTW path start function** (Fig. 5, mapping each end point back to a start location — flat/long steps mean many end points converge on the same start, i.e., a strong repeated candidate).

3. **Feature extraction + classifier (§3.3–3.4).** From the surviving candidate start locations, 13 features are computed per location (min/avg/std DTW cost across end points; path length, slope, and perpendicular deviation from the "idealized" straight-line path, again per end point) and fed to a 200-tree random forest.

**Results (§5, Tables 1–2, on their own 80-sample/80-song public dataset):** location-level ("micro") precision 79.4%, recall 34.6%, F-measure 48.2%; song-level ("macro") precision 83.3%, recall 50.0%, F-measure 62.5%. AUROC 72.5%. The paper is explicit about why recall is low: extreme class imbalance (289 true sample-instances vs. ~74,000 negative candidate windows in the test set, Table 2) and roughly 7% of true instances lost outright at the candidate-selection stage because "the DTW cost function did not have a minimum at respective sample locations" (§5). §6 explicitly scopes the paper as a *pre-processing aid for human musicologists/forensic reviewers*, not an autonomous, real-time system.

## 2. Mechanism-by-problem mapping

### (a) Harmonic comb ambiguity — 1259 ms overshoot, on-beat-but-3-beats-late lock, 47.6 s Billie Jean phantom offset

**What applies, and how concretely:**

- **Wang §2.3/§2.3.1's histogram-of-δt / dominant-cluster test is the right *shape* of fix, but we cannot apply it to ACRCloud's output** — ACRCloud (per docs/recognition-audit-2026.md) hands us one `(track, offset_ms, score)` triple, not the underlying scatterplot of matched landmark pairs. We are the *consumer* of a Wang-style engine, not the implementer, for the network fix path. Wang's mechanism is only borrowable where **we own both signals being compared** — which is true for `lag_window.h`'s `analyze_window` (autocorrelation of our own mixed capture) and for the self-match problem in (b), not for the ACRCloud fix.
- **What genuinely transfers to `analyze_window`:** `lag_window.h` currently reduces a full lag-range autocorrelation to a single `WindowLag{lag_ms, peak_ratio, found}` — i.e., it already computes the harmonic comb (the full correlation curve across the search range) and then throws away every peak except the winner. Wang §2.3.1's insight — that a *score relative to the distribution of competing scores* is what separates a real match from noise, not the raw peak alone — is directly implementable here at near-zero marginal cost: the FFT-domain correlation array in `analyze_window` already exists; extracting the top-K local maxima instead of `argmax` and reporting the ratio between the 1st and 2nd-best peak is the same data, differently reduced. This is a **comb-flatness / ambiguity score**, not currently exposed anywhere in the estimator or policy layer.
- **Gururani & Lerch's subsequence-DTW candidate survival (§3.2.2, Eq. 2, Fig. 3–5) is the closer structural analog of the actual failure**, because their Fig. 3 (looped sample) is exactly our situation: one true underlying signal, multiple structurally-valid alignments at periodic offsets. Their answer is instructive by contrast: **they do not disambiguate which occurrence is "the" one — they report all of them** and export per-candidate features (survival count, path straightness) for something downstream (a classifier, in their case; a human, per §6) to adjudicate. The transferable idea for us is not their NMF/DTW machinery (see §3, cost) but the *pattern*: keep multiple offset hypotheses alive with a per-hypothesis survival/agreement count, and let corroboration over time — not a single fix — collapse the set. That pattern already exists in embryonic form in `estimator.h`'s outlier gate (`outlier_gate_ms`, `outlier_gate_max_p00`): "a SECOND consecutive outlier is accepted — a real jump repeats, noise doesn't." Extending one implicit two-state gate into a small explicit bank of hypotheses is a generalization of code we already have, not a new paradigm.
- **What it would cost:** extending `WindowLag` to a fixed-size top-K struct is a small, contained change to `lag_window.h` — the file's own header warns "do not 'improve' the math here without re-running the field-test corpus," so this still needs a full corpus re-run, but the DSP cost is negligible (no new FFT, no new buffers, just a different reduction over data already computed). A multi-hypothesis Kalman bank (2–3 parallel copies of the existing 2-state `SyncEstimator`, sharing player-state/seek inputs, one instantiated per comb-tooth candidate) is more code but reuses `estimator.h` verbatim — no DTW, no NMF. **The one piece neither paper supplies and we would have to build ourselves: a tempo/beat estimate to know the comb spacing** (bar/beat length in ms) at which to seed candidate hypotheses. Wang has no beat tracker (his tracks are matched as opaque hash sets, no rhythmic model); Gururani & Lerch don't need one either (their DTW candidates emerge from the alignment itself, not from a prior beat-spacing model). This is a real gap, not a borrowed win.

### (b) Self-match after room discontinuity — perfect 1:1 continuity, constant raw error, every telemetry layer self-confirms

**What applies, and how concretely:**

- **This is conceptually the strongest fit of either paper — but its premise fails on this codebase, and the correction matters.** Wang §3.1's "transparency" property describes exactly the discriminative power we are missing: telling, from one mixed mic capture, whether a specific *known* signal is present in it. The mechanism (§2.1–2.3: constellation peaks → combinatorial hashes → δt-histogram against a single-track index) is cheap and would be a **direct** self-match test, superior to CTL-01's absence-of-evidence proxy.
- **CORRECTED (review, same day): we do not possess the reference audio.** Spotify's app renders the music; this app has no PCM access to its own playback — the identical phantom-reference assumption that invalidated the referee's first cross-correlation spec (see the `sc_push_reference` correction in technical-requirements.md §2.6). The only chain-of-evidence paths to a reference, each with a hard caveat:
  - `AudioPlaybackCapture` (Android 10+) captures other apps' output **only if the target app allows it**, and major media apps commonly opt out — whether Spotify's manifest permits capture must be verified on-device before ANY design assumes this exists.
  - `Visualizer` on session 0 exposes the output mix, but as low-rate, low-bit snapshots designed for visualizations — almost certainly insufficient for landmark fingerprinting; would need on-device evaluation before being believed.
  - **An active probe needs no reference at all**: we control our own playback, so a deliberate, known perturbation (a ~200 ms pause blip or micro-seek) is a signature only OUR audio carries. If the recognizer's "continuous" timeline follows the perturbation, it is tracking us; if it doesn't, it is tracking the room. This is not from either paper — it is the buildable substitute this correction forces, and it composes with CTL-01's referee proxy rather than replacing it.
  So Wang's self-fingerprint stands as the design to reach for **iff** a reference source is ever verified on-device; until then (b)'s buildable answers are the active probe + CTL-01's referee sentinel.
- **Cost to implement:** this is the cheapest of the borrowed mechanisms, because the "database" is one track, not Wang's 1.8M. `correlate.h`/`lag_window.h` already run kissfft-based spectral analysis over the 12 s post-AEC capture ring; adding local-maxima peak-picking over that same STFT output (§2.1: "higher energy than all neighbors in a region centered around the point") is incremental. Combinatorial hashing (§2.2: anchor + target-zone pairing → 32-bit `[f1:f2:Δt]`) against a **single track's own landmark list** (order 10³–10⁴ hashes for a 3–5 minute song, trivial for phone RAM) needs only a small sorted array or `unordered_multimap`, not a production search index. Matching and histogramming (§2.3) is described by the paper itself as running in "on the order of microseconds per bin" even against a 20,000-track database (§3.2) — against one track it is negligible. This is genuinely buildable in the existing C++17/kissfft core with no ML runtime, as a new sibling module to `correlate.h` (e.g. `self_fingerprint.h`), estimated at a few hundred lines plus test corpus validation.
- **Gururani & Lerch's PFNMF (§3.1) is a theoretically cleaner fit for the harder version of this problem** — separating a known reference signal out of an *unknown* polyphonic mixture (room track + our bleed, simultaneously) rather than assuming one dominates — but is **too costly to run in real time on a phone**: their pipeline runs 12 separate PFNMF factorizations (one per pitch-shift hypothesis) plus subsequence DTW per song candidate, on file-length audio, offline, feeding a 200-tree classifier (§3.4). Pitch-shift robustness itself is irrelevant to us (Spotify playback isn't detuned), so at minimum we'd drop that 12x loop — but even a single NMF+PFNMF+DTW pass per analysis window is a heavier, iterative, non-closed-form computation than anything currently in the DSP core (GCC-PHAT and windowed autocorrelation are both closed-form FFT operations; NMF is iterative multiplicative-update optimization). Flagged as **not recommended** for the phone CPU budget; noted for completeness because it is the theoretically more general answer to "which source is which" (§2 revisits this in the cost section).

### (c) Deadband-locked residuals — constant ~300 ms (engine 281 / mic 314 / ear ~250), deadband-150 caused 8 corrections in 77 s

**What applies, and how concretely:** neither paper meaningfully addresses this. Both are single-shot, batch matching problems (a 15 s clip against a static database; a whole song against a whole sample file) — neither has an online recursive estimator, a notion of measurement noise floor, or a control-loop deadband/correction-gating policy. The closest tangential material:

- Wang §2.3.1's false-positive threshold ("an acceptable false positive rate is chosen... then a threshold score is chosen that meets or exceeds the false-positive criterion") is a *decision-threshold-from-empirical-distribution* pattern, structurally similar to how a deadband is chosen, but it's tuned once offline against a database of non-matches — it says nothing about a *live, constant, agreed-upon-by-three-instruments* residual, which is our actual (c) case. That is a genuinely different problem: three independent measurements (engine 281 ms / mic 314 ms / ear ~250 ms) already agree, so there's no correlation-ambiguity to disambiguate — the open question is a control-policy one (is a corroborated small residual a real error worth correcting, or an unmodeled acoustic-path floor?), and neither paper models an acoustic path at all.
- If anything, a **precision gain from (a)'s comb-flatness score** could indirectly help here: a tighter per-fix confidence measure (dominant-peak-to-second-peak ratio, borrowed from Wang §2.3.1's scoring logic) could, in principle, let the deadband shrink safely for *high-confidence* fixes only, rather than uniformly — but this is speculative composition, not something either paper demonstrates, and the field-test corpus already showed uniform deadband-lowering fails (the deadband-150 experiment: "eight corrections in 77 s chasing the song's own beat comb into an on-beat-but-3-beats-late hole," field-test-8-results.md). **This problem stays entirely in the control-loop/CTL-02 domain**; see §3.

## 3. What the papers do NOT solve for us — explicit list

1. **Neither paper is an online/streaming estimator.** No Kalman filter, no drift-ppm state, no notion of a `seek_exec_var_ms2`-style variance injection on a local seek. `estimator.h`'s entire timing model (§6.1 in architecture-spec, `error_ms`/`drift_ms_per_s`, innovation gating, convergence streak) has no counterpart in either paper — both are single-shot batch matches. Anything about *maintaining* an estimate across time, corroborating fixes, or gating corrections by size is ours to design; the papers only inform what a single fix's *evidence quality* looks like.
2. **Neither paper distinguishes "the same signal from two sources at different lags" as a first-class case.** Wang's "transparency" (§3.1) is demonstrated for *distinct* tracks mixed together; our self-match problem is the *harder* case of the identical signal appearing twice (near-duplicate, not distinct) — landmark hash collisions are now guaranteed rather than merely possible, which is a strictly worse condition for hash specificity than anything Wang's false-positive analysis (§2.3.1) models. Applicability is real (§2b) but the paper doesn't validate it for this exact scenario — we would be extending, not directly applying, the technique.
3. **Neither paper offers threshold/deadband tuning guidance, corroboration policy, or any equivalent of CTL-02's "large corrections need a second agreeing fix."** That is entirely our own control-systems design, already underway (field-test-8-results.md's ticketed CTL-01/CTL-02).
4. **Gururani & Lerch's own numbers are a explicit caution against over-trusting the general "multi-hypothesis" approach**: even offline, with full-file NMF+DTW and a trained classifier, their location-level recall is 34.6% (Table 1) and ~7% of true instances are lost before classification even runs (§5) — "most problematic cases... were hard to detect for humans as well." We should borrow the *pattern* (keep multiple candidates alive, let survival counts disambiguate) but claim no performance transfer from their numbers; our domain (real-time, one track, high-SNR near-field mic) is materially easier than theirs (offline, arbitrary polyphonic mixes, adversarial mixing), but also has a much tighter latency budget they never had to meet.
5. **Neither paper models loudspeaker+room+mic acoustic coloration.** Wang's robustness testing (§3.1, Figs. 4–5) is against additive noise and GSM 6.10 codec compression — not multipath/reverb from a co-located device speaker and mic, which is our dominant real channel condition and already has bespoke handling in `lag_window.h`'s comment on mild-whitening-vs-full-PHAT. Gururani & Lerch never touch acoustic transmission at all (their "song" and "sample" are both clean digital audio). This domain knowledge is already ours; neither paper adds to it.
6. **Pitch-shift/time-stretch robustness — a major fraction of Gururani & Lerch's design (§3.2.2, the 12-hypothesis pitch-shift loop) — is irrelevant to us** and should not be ported; Spotify-class playback doesn't detune or retime. Explicitly dropping it is part of the cost savings noted in §2b, not a loss.
7. **Neither paper gives us a beat/tempo tracker.** As noted in §2a, a comb-spacing-aware multi-hypothesis scheme needs to know the bar/beat length in ms to seed candidates; neither Wang (opaque hash sets, no rhythmic model) nor Gururani & Lerch (candidates emerge from DTW alignment, not a rhythm prior) supply one. This is a real, separate build item if we pursue the multi-hypothesis direction in §2a.

## 4. Recommendation

**Smallest combination that addresses (a) + (b) + (c), composed with the existing referee/CTL-01/CTL-02 direction:**

1. **Build a self-reference fingerprinter (Wang §2.1–2.3, "transparency" §3.1) as a new direct evidence source for CTL-01.** Fingerprint only the currently-loaded track's own rendered audio (we own it exactly — no acquisition problem), match against the existing 12 s post-AEC capture ring via constellation + combinatorial hash + δt-histogram (near-zero incremental DSP cost on top of the STFT already run for `lag_window.h`/`correlate.h`). This gives an **explicit, positive self-match signal** — "I hear my own output at lag X with hash-vote score N" — to sit alongside the referee's *indirect* "validity collapsed" signal from CTL-01. Two independent detectors (one presence-based, one absence-based) closing the same hole is strictly stronger than either alone, and directly answers ux-notes #10/#11's observation that self-match after a room discontinuity fools every existing telemetry layer because "our own playback seamlessly continues the old room timeline."

2. **Extend `lag_window.h`'s `WindowLag` to expose a comb-flatness / peak-ambiguity score (Wang §2.3.1's scoring logic, generalized), and route it into CTL-02's corroboration gate.** Instead of "large corrections need a second agreeing fix" applied uniformly, gate on **evidence quality**: a fix from a sharp, dominant, unambiguous peak can be trusted closer to Wang's single-token confidence; a fix from a flat comb (multiple near-equal peaks — Billie Jean's 47.6 s phantom offset, the 1259 ms overshoot's likely "self-similar intro riff matching one phrase off" per ux-notes #8) always requires corroboration, regardless of correction size. This reuses the existing `outlier_gate_ms`/`outlier_gate_max_p00` machinery in `estimator.h` rather than inventing new estimator state.

3. **Do not build Gururani & Lerch's actual NMF+PFNMF+DTW pipeline** — too expensive for the phone CPU budget and solving a different (offline, polyphonic-mixture, pitch-shift-robust) problem than ours — **but borrow the multi-hypothesis-survival pattern** (§3.2.2's subsequence DTW keeping every backtracking candidate alive, Fig. 5) as the eventual (a) disambiguation layer: a small fixed bank of parallel `SyncEstimator` instances (existing code, no new DSP), seeded at comb-implied candidate offsets, pruned by ordinary corroboration until one survives. This is explicitly the multi-hypothesis-tracking direction ux-notes #14 already named as a candidate; this research gives it a citable precedent and a cost-bounded implementation path (reuse `estimator.h`, no DTW/NMF), while flagging its one real prerequisite — a tempo/beat estimate to define comb spacing — as new work neither paper supplies.

4. **Leave the deadband/residual problem (c) to CTL-02 and the control loop alone.** Neither paper offers a mechanism for it; the one indirect connection (tighter per-fix confidence from item 2, above, potentially enabling confidence-conditional deadband relaxation someday) is speculative and should not be scheduled ahead of the corroboration work already ticketed.

**Net:** items 1 and 2 are cheap, contained, and directly buildable now in the existing C++17/kissfft core with no new dependencies; item 3 is a real but bounded follow-on that depends on a beat tracker we don't have yet; item 4 stays out of scope for these two papers entirely.
